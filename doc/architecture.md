# zstep architecture

zstep is a step/dir stepper motion controller for Zephyr, aimed at CNC machines and 3D printers. It implements the `stepper_ctrl` driver API of Zephyr v4.4.2.

## 1. Scope

- Motion of each motor: trapezoidal acceleration ramps with separate acceleration, deceleration and speed limits. Targets, direction and speed may change while the motor runs.
- Several motors on one hardware counter, each on its own alarm channel, with independent timing.
- Synchronized group moves: several axes start on the same counter tick with proportionally scaled profiles, so the tool moves along a straight line.
- Input shaping against the resonance of the frame.

The driver chip (enable, micro-step resolution) stays with the upstream `stepper` drivers, such as `ti,drv84xx`, `allegro,a4979` and `adi,tmc2209`. zstep only generates the step and direction signals.

Excluded: a look-ahead planner (a sequence of group moves stops between segments), kinematics, closed-loop control.

## 2. Packaging and layout

The repository is a Zephyr module and a west T2 manifest repository, following zephyrproject-rtos/example-application. `west.yml` pins Zephyr v4.4.2 without its modules, which is enough for the tests and the sample on native_sim. A firmware adds zstep to its own manifest without importing this one and brings the HALs of its boards. `zephyr/module.yml` sets `build.cmake: .`, `build.kconfig: Kconfig` and `build.settings.dts_root: .` for the `zstep,step-dir` binding.

## 3. Conventions

- Positions are in micro-steps, speeds in micro-steps/s, accelerations in micro-steps/s^2, as in the `stepper_ctrl` API.
- Time is counted in ticks of the hardware counter of the motor.
- Devicetree properties carry their unit in the name: `-ns`, `-centihz`, `-permille`.
- The step interrupt uses no floating point, and the driver does not busy-wait.

## 4. Step generation

Each motor keeps its own time grid: the next edge is due at the previous edge plus the interval. The grid is never re-based on the moment an interrupt was served. If an interrupt runs late, only that edge moves, and the following edges keep their times, so the move is not stretched and the latency caused by other motors does not accumulate.

The edges are generated by single-shot alarms of a free-running counter, one alarm channel per motor. A motor never waits for another one. The counter must count over a power-of-two range and allow an alarm to be re-armed from its own callback. The longest interval is half the counter range.

On single-edge drivers a step costs two interrupts. The next interval is computed while the step pulse is high, so the computation stays off the step edge. Drivers that step on both edges (`dual-edge-step`, e.g. TMC22xx) need one interrupt per step. `step-width-ns` and `dual-edge-step` are taken from the `stepper-driver` node when it has them. `dir-setup-ns` separates a DIR change from the next step edge.

## 5. Ramp

The profile uses the real-time algorithm of D. Austin, with two changes:

- The first two intervals are computed exactly.
- A look-ahead stop rule plans braking in integer energy terms (v^2), so stops, retargets and reversals end exactly on the target step.

Intervals are fixed-point numbers of counter ticks with 8 fraction bits (`ZSTEP_RAMP_Q`), which keeps the average speed exact with a 1 MHz counter. A step costs one 32-bit division.

Without an acceleration limit the motor runs at constant speed. A target behind the motor makes it brake, reverse and come back. The ramp limits change only at rest. Events raised in an ISR are delivered from the system work queue with `CONFIG_STEPPER_CTRL_ISR_SAFE_EVENTS`.

## 6. Group moves

`zstep_group_move_by()` takes idle axes on one counter and arms all of them for the same counter tick, `CONFIG_ZSTEP_GROUP_START_US` in the future. The axis with the longest move follows the limits. `zstep_profile_scale()` slows the other axes down in proportion to their share of the move, so the axes stay proportional over time and the tool moves along a straight line. Every axis reports `STEPPER_CTRL_EVENT_STEPS_COMPLETED` itself. A sequence of group moves stops between segments.

## 7. Testing

- `tests/unit/ramp` compares every step against the analytic trapezoid, requires moves of 1 to 2000 steps to end on target and covers braking distance, speed changes, reversal, limits and the straightness of a group.
- `tests/integration/conformance` runs the upstream `stepper_ctrl` API suite of the Zephyr tree against the driver.
- `tests/integration/timing` records every GPIO edge with its counter tick (`src/gpio_spy.c`) and checks pulse width and DIR setup and hold, bit-exact step times, that the edges of a motor are identical whether it runs alone or together with others, and the group start on one tick.
- `samples/multi_axis` runs on native_sim with a console harness.
- CI runs on Woodpecker (`.woodpecker/build.yaml`): twister runs the tests and the sample on `native_sim` and `native_sim/native/64` and builds the sample for `nucleo_f446re`, `nrf52840dk/nrf52840` and `rpi_pico` in a second workspace with the manifest of Zephyr v4.4.2 and only the HALs of these boards. The step `docs` builds the documentation of `include/zstep` with `doxygen doc/Doxyfile`, which fails on any warning.
